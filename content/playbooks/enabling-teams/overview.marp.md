# Enabling Teams

Plays for helping a team gain a capability, then leaving

---

## Agree the exit signal before the first session

**When:** nobody, including you, can say what "done" would look like.

**The action:** agree one sign the team will be able to see when they no longer need you. Write it down where they can see it.

**Signals:** the team refers to the exit signal unprompted.

---

## Hand the third repeat to the platform team

**When:** you are about to teach the same thing to a third team.

**The action:** stop teaching it. Raise it with the platform team as a capability they could offer self-service.

**Signals:** the next team gets the capability without contacting you.

---

## Decline when the gap is capacity rather than capability

**When:** the team already knows how to do the thing. What they lack is time.

**The action:** say no, and send the conversation back to whoever sets the team's priorities.

**Signals:** the team's priorities change, and your queue holds capability requests.

---

## Run an inception when the team can't state the problem the same way twice

**When:** three people give three different answers, and nobody seems bothered.

**The action:** agree the problem, the people it affects, and what the team will not do. Keep it to days.

**Signals:** the team describes the problem in matching terms within a week.

---

## Harvest a session into a module after its second run

**When:** you have run the same session for a second team, and it worked both times.

**The action:** harvest it into a reusable module. Capture what changed between the two runs.

**Signals:** someone else runs the module without asking you how.

---

## Switch to dialogue when the same argument comes back a third time

**When:** people restate their positions more firmly each round and nobody learns anything new.

**The action:** name the pattern. Ask each side to explain their assumptions until the others can state them back.

**Signals:** people restate each other's assumptions accurately.

---

## Reflect back before proposing when a team rejects your first suggestion

**When:** the team pushed back and you want to offer a better idea straight away.

**The action:** hold the next idea. Play back why the first one didn't fit, and check you have it right.

**Signals:** the team corrects or adds to your playback.

---

## Coaching skills belong in training modules

A play can point to a module when a team lacks a skill like active listening. It doesn't teach the skill itself.
