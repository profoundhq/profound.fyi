The playbook draws on several traditions. None of the arguments are original; the synthesis is.

## Primary debts

**W. Edwards Deming** - *Out of the Crisis* (1986) and *The New Economics* (1993). The System of Profound Knowledge is the lens behind the entire playbook. Most of what is uncomfortable in the argument is uncomfortable because Deming was right about it in 1986 and the management literature has been catching up slowly ever since.

**John Doerr** - *Measure What Matters* (2018). The canonical OKR text. The commit-aspire distinction is Doerr's; the playbook treats it as more important than most rollouts do.

**Christina Wodtke** - *Radical Focus* (2016). The argument that OKRs require rhythm to survive comes from Wodtke. So does the position, mostly implicit here, that one Objective per team per quarter is what discipline actually looks like.

**John Cutler** - North Star Framework writings (2017 onwards). The argument that Key Results should be leading indicators of a user-value metric is Cutler's. The position that this is structurally anti-cascade is the playbook's extension.

**Felipe Castro** - *The Beginner's Guide to OKRs* and subsequent writings. Castro is the clearest contemporary voice on what cascading does to OKRs and why it usually shouldn't.

**Bart den Haak** - *Moving the Needle With Lean OKRs* (2021). The fullest treatment of OKRs as alignment-through-contribution rather than alignment-through-decomposition.

**Daniel Pink** - *Drive* (2009). The synthesis of self-determination theory and motivation research that underpins the psychology section. The argument that extrinsic incentives degrade complex performance is Pink's; the application to OKR practice is the playbook's.

## Indirect debts

**Dave Snowden and the wider Cynefin community.** The constraint-based vocabulary in the interventions section borrows from Cynefin . Enabling constraints, dispositional change, attractor patterns - these are Snowden's terms, used in the playbook without their underlying ontology being developed. The hedge is acknowledged in the open questions section.

**Donella Meadows** - *Thinking in Systems* (2008) and the *Leverage Points* essay. The argument that interventions at the level of system goals and paradigms produce more leverage than interventions at the level of parameters and feedback delays runs through the entire playbook.

**Russell Ackoff** - *Idealised Design* and the wider Ackoff corpus. The position that most organisational problems are messes rather than puzzles, and that messes do not yield to analysis alone, is Ackoff's.

**Mike Rother** - *Toyota Kata* (2010). The improvement-kata cycle informs the rhythm constraint without being named in the body of the playbook.

## The wider tradition

The playbook sits broadly within the **sociotechnical tradition**. Eric Trist, Fred Emery, the Tavistock Institute, and the line of work that connects them to modern Team Topologies and DORA. The position that systems of work are inseparable from systems of people, and that you cannot redesign one without the other, is the sociotechnical commitment that runs through the argument.

It also sits adjacent to the **DevOps and continuous delivery tradition** - Jez Humble, Dave Farley, Gene Kim and the State of DevOps reports. The OKR question of how measurement shapes behaviour rhymes with the DevOps question of how feedback loops shape capability.

## What is original

Three positions are the playbook's own.

The framing of OKRs as a management philosophy with a template attached, rather than a tool. The literature comes close to this - Wodtke and Castro both argue that OKRs encode worldview - but the explicit framing here is the playbook's.

The four signals as diagnostic rather than prescriptive. Most OKR teaching treats failure patterns as problems to fix. The playbook treats them as evidence to read. This shift is not without precedent - it is the standard move of clinical diagnosis applied to organisational practice - but it is not standard in the OKR literature.

The mapping of the three constraints to the four signals via the two functions. The constraints, the signals, and the functions are each individually present in the existing literature. The systematic mapping between them is the playbook's contribution.
