The thinking here stands on:

- **Chris Argyris and Donald Schön**, for the espoused theory and theory-in-use distinction that anchors the extraction discipline.
- **Toyota's standard work and yokoten**, and **Mike Rother's Toyota Kata**, for the resolution of the codification and improvement tension: the standard as baseline, not ceiling.
- **Edgar Schein**, for the layers of culture that explain why a playbook cannot codify "how we do things around here."
- **Christopher Alexander**, and the software pattern tradition through **Gamma, Helm, Johnson, and Vlissides**, **James Coplien**, and **Mary Lynn Manns and Linda Rising**, for the pattern form: context, forces, resolution, consequences.
- **Stafford Beer**, for POSIWID, which applies to the playbook system as much as to any other.
- **W. Edwards Deming**, for the reminder that an aspiration without a method is a slogan.
- **The RFC and ADR traditions**, for lightweight decision records carried with the artefact.
- **GDS's guidance model for the Service Manual** (Harrison and Miller, 2018), for the two-reader problem, progressive disclosure via exposing the why, and the defence of discretion against the demand for one-size-fits-all certainty.
- **Public Digital**, whose recommendations to Cambridge Assessment on treating a playbook as a product, distributed authorship, and publishing in the open supplied three candidate plays and independent confirmation of several others.

Errors of interpretation are mine.
