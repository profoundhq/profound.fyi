**Codification standardises. Improvement requires variation. Most playbooks never resolve that tension.**

They die one of two deaths: a museum piece nobody follows, or an enforcement tool that improvement has to fight. Laminate your ways of working, and you get the second death quickly. The artefact meant to spread good practice becomes the thing teams route around.

The resolution is the one Toyota reached with standard work. The standard is not the ceiling. It is the baseline that makes improvement visible. You cannot see a local improvement without a standard to see it against.

This only holds if two conditions are met. Every play is explicitly provisional. And there is a cheap, known path for a local improvement to become the new standard.

That path is the most important play in this playbook. It lives in [Govern](/playbooks/creating-playbooks/govern/), and everything else here feeds it. A playbook without that loop is a museum piece on day one, whatever its preface says.

The implication is uncomfortable but necessary: the writing is the least interesting part of creating a playbook. The system around the writing, sensing what to codify, extracting it honestly, and governing how it changes, is where playbooks succeed or fail.
