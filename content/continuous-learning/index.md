A catalogue of social and technical practices that can support continuous, in-work learning, with an emphasis on learning together. Explore it on your own or with your team.

This is a catalogue, and it is not a playbook. Each entry says what a practice is and what to watch out for. None of them yet says when the practice applies, when to avoid it, or how you would know it is working.

## Practices

Each practice has its own page.

- [1-1 coaching](/continuous-learning/one-to-one-coaching/). A coach works with one person over a series of conversations to help them think through goals, problems, and their own ways of working.
- [Asking for help](/continuous-learning/asking-for-help/). Sounds straightforward, yet people often avoid asking for help, for many reasons.
- [Blameless post-mortems](/continuous-learning/blameless-post-mortems/). After an incident or failure, the people involved meet to reconstruct what happened, why their actions made sense at the time, and what to change.
- [Blogging](/continuous-learning/blogging/). Writing, internally or in public, about what you are working on and learning.
- [Book clubs](/continuous-learning/book-clubs/). A group reads the same book and meets regularly to discuss it.
- [Brainstorming](/continuous-learning/brainstorming/). A group generates many ideas quickly before judging any of them.
- [Chatrooms and chatbots](/continuous-learning/chatrooms-and-chatbots/). Chat tools let people ask questions and share what they know in the flow of work.
- [Code review](/continuous-learning/code-review/). Someone other than the author reads a change before it is merged.
- [Communities of practice](/continuous-learning/communities-of-practice/). People who share a craft or interest across team boundaries meet regularly to learn from each other, for example testers, designers, or people working with a particular technology.
- [Conferences](/continuous-learning/conferences/). Attending or speaking at conferences exposes people to ideas, tools, and peers beyond their own organisation.
- [Creating shared resources](/continuous-learning/creating-shared-resources/). Teams build learning materials together, such as guides, templates, checklists, diagrams, or example code.
- [Demos](/continuous-learning/demos/). A team shows working software or other outputs to stakeholders and colleagues, usually at the end of an iteration.
- [Documenting](/continuous-learning/documenting/). Writing down how things work, why decisions were taken, and how to do common tasks.
- [Dojos](/continuous-learning/dojos/). A dojo is a dedicated space and time to practise skills on realistic problems, away from delivery pressure.
- [Experiments](/continuous-learning/experiments/). Treating a change as an experiment, with a hypothesis, a small test, and a way to measure the result.
- [Game days and simulations](/continuous-learning/game-days-and-simulations/). A team rehearses a realistic scenario, such as a system outage or a security incident, in a controlled setting.
- [Giving and receiving feedback](/continuous-learning/giving-and-receiving-feedback/). Telling someone how their work or behaviour affected you, and hearing the same about yourself.
- [Group coaching](/continuous-learning/group-coaching/). A coach works with a small group of people, often with similar roles or challenges, who are not necessarily in the same team.
- [Group problem-solving](/continuous-learning/group-problem-solving/). People with different perspectives work on a problem together, using a structured method such as root cause analysis, the five whys, or A3 thinking.
- [Guided experiences](/continuous-learning/guided-experiences/). A guided experience is a curated sequence of tasks that takes a person through a multistage process, possibly spanning several systems.
- [Hack days](/continuous-learning/hack-days/). A day or two set aside for people to work on ideas of their own choosing, often in small cross-team groups.
- [Improvement blitzes](/continuous-learning/improvement-blitzes/). A short, focused burst of effort, typically a few days, in which a cross-functional group improves a specific process or problem.
- [Learning backlog](/continuous-learning/learning-backlog/). A visible list of things a person or team wants to learn, managed alongside the work backlog.
- [Lightning talks](/continuous-learning/lightning-talks/). A lightning talk is a short talk, usually lasting two to five minutes.
- [Lunch and learns](/continuous-learning/lunch-and-learns/). If you are expecting people to give up their lunchtime, choose the activities and format sensitively so people have the chance to eat and draw breath.
- [Mentoring](/continuous-learning/mentoring/). A more experienced person supports someone's development through regular conversations, sharing advice, experience, and connections.
- [Mobbing](/continuous-learning/mobbing/). The whole team works on the same thing, at the same time, at the same computer.
- [Observability tools](/continuous-learning/observability-tools/). Logs, metrics, traces, and dashboards show how systems behave in production.
- [Online courses](/continuous-learning/online-courses/). Self-paced or scheduled courses delivered online, from short tutorials to full certifications.
- [Pairing](/continuous-learning/pairing/). Pairing means working in partnership with someone, in real time, towards the same goal.
- [Peer review](/continuous-learning/peer-review/). Colleagues review each other's work, such as documents, designs, plans, or analysis, before it is finalised.
- [Processing pauses](/continuous-learning/processing-pauses/). A processing pause means taking time to think, or simply to be, after an emotionally or cognitively demanding experience.
- [Reading](/continuous-learning/reading/). Reading books, articles, papers, and documentation to learn independently.
- [Reflective questioning](/continuous-learning/reflective-questioning/). A powerful learning skill that anyone can use to prompt reflection in others.
- [Retrospectives](/continuous-learning/retrospectives/). A team regularly pauses to reflect on how it has been working and agree what to change.
- [Shared source code repository](/continuous-learning/shared-source-code-repository/). Making code visible across teams so anyone can read it, learn from it, and contribute, an approach often called inner source.
- [Special interest channels](/continuous-learning/special-interest-channels/). Chat channels dedicated to a topic, technology, or interest, open to anyone in the organisation.
- [Swarming](/continuous-learning/swarming/). The team stops starting new work and concentrates on finishing a single item, often an urgent problem or a blocked piece of work.
- [Team coaching](/continuous-learning/team-coaching/). A coach works with a whole team to improve how it works together, including communication, decision-making, and delivery practices.
- [Team health checks](/continuous-learning/team-health-checks/). A team regularly rates itself against a set of factors, such as pace, quality, fun, learning, and support, and discusses the results.
- [Tests as documentation](/continuous-learning/tests-as-documentation/). Well-written automated tests describe how a system is expected to behave, with examples that stay current because they run.
- [Today I learned](/continuous-learning/today-i-learned/). People share small things they have learned, in a dedicated channel, a shared document, or a regular slot in a meeting.
- [Unconference](/continuous-learning/unconference/). A participant-led event where attendees set the agenda on the day.
- [Walk and talks](/continuous-learning/walk-and-talks/). A walk and talk is a conversation away from the desk.
- [Watercooler chats](/continuous-learning/watercooler-chats/). Informal, unplanned conversations between colleagues.
- [Wiki or knowledge base](/continuous-learning/wiki-or-knowledge-base/). A shared, searchable store of information that anyone can read and update.
- [Work shadowing](/continuous-learning/work-shadowing/). Spending time observing someone in a different role or team as they do their work.
- [Workshops](/continuous-learning/workshops/). A facilitated session where participants work actively on a topic or problem together, instead of listening to a presentation.
