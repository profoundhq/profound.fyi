**How to use it.** Use it for non-trivial work that needs careful thinking, knowledge transfer, or onboarding. The two people should be of comparable engagement, if not skill. The keyboard rotates. The conversation matters more than the typing.

**How it gets misused.** Two people watching one screen for a day is something else. Pair programming needs the role swap and both people actively engaged. Done badly, it doubles the cost and halves the throughput. Done well, it produces work that needs less rework and less review, and it onboards people faster than any other method.
